Summary Report, Tom
Written for Tom

A score of 25/100 at age 30 reflects a physique that is currently well below what the research identifies as optimal for your age group. This is not a cosmetic judgment. Studies consistently show that the physical cues driving attractiveness assessments, including body composition, torso shape, and proportional development, operate as reliable signals of health and physical capacity [Sell, Lukaszewski & Townsley, 2017; Lassek & Gaulin, 2009]. At your current composition, those signals are largely obscured. The good news is that the score reflects a starting point with significant structural room to move, and your posture is already within the optimal range, which is a genuine foundation to build from.

The most significant issue is body fat. At approximately 23%, you are carrying enough excess fat to flatten the torso contours that drive attractiveness perception, particularly the visible distinction between chest, waist, and hips. Research confirms that perceived upper body strength accounts for around 70% of the variance in male bodily attractiveness ratings [Sell, Lukaszewski & Townsley, 2017], and that signal cannot register when it is buried under a soft midsection.

Directly connected to this is your current torso taper, which sits well below the inverted-triangle shape identified as a primary attractiveness cue across multiple study designs [Maisey et al., 1999; Hughes & Gallup, 2003]. In plain terms, your shoulders and chest do not yet read as meaningfully wider than your waist, which collapses the visual hierarchy that makes a male physique register as physically capable. These two factors compound each other: reducing fat and building the upper frame simultaneously addresses both.

The gap between 25 and 100 is large, but it is almost entirely recoverable. Your realistic target places you at a body fat closer to 14%, with a chest-to-waist ratio and shoulder width that create a clear V-taper visible through smart casual clothing. That combination is precisely what reads as disciplined and high-functioning in an entrepreneurial context, where your physique is seen across boardroom settings and informal team environments alike. You are not building toward an extreme. The research is clear that moderate muscularity, not maximal size, is what signals genetic quality and social competence [Frederick & Haselton, 2007; Dixson et al., 2003].

The physique you are building toward photographs well in a fitted shirt, carries authority in a room, and requires no explanation in any context you operate in.

At 30, with no structural limitations and a clear ceiling defined by the research, this is the most efficient window you will have to close that gap, and the changes required are concrete, sequenced, and fully within reach.

Annex · Height · A.01
191 cmTall · above the range where height drives the strongest gains

Height correlates consistently with male attractiveness across cultures [Nettle 2002; Pawlowski 2000; Stulp 2013]. The effect is real but non-linear: gains are strongest below ~183 cm and plateau above it. Height is roughly 80% heritable and established in early adulthood; no protocol changes skeletal height.

What you control: postural alignment (the PAS score) directly affects perceived height. Forward head posture and thoracic kyphosis can reduce apparent height by 2–5 cm. Every point gained on PAS is effectively a height gain in the perception of observers, without adding a centimeter to the skeleton.

Annex · Behavioral Signals · A.02

Physical metrics capture the static baseline. Dynamic signals, movement, gaze, voice, and spatial behavior, are processed in real social contexts and can amplify or undermine the impression created by the physique.

Gait & movement

Deliberate, unhurried movement is read as a high-status signal within 180ms of observation [Grammer 1996]. Walking at ~80% of normal pace is one of the highest-leverage behavioral adjustments.

Eye contact

Sustained gaze (3–5 second holds before breaking) signals social confidence. Immediate gaze aversion reads as low status.

Voice

Lower pitch and slower tempo are associated with perceived dominance [Puts 2005]. Resonance (chest voice) matters more than raw pitch. Deliberate pauses before answering signal confidence.

Spatial behavior

Expansive posture, wide stance, open positioning, reinforces the same dominance cues that make PAS valuable in photos. Contracting the body signals low status regardless of physique.

At 6–7 hours with no fixed schedule, Tom is likely running a chronic sleep debt that suppresses anabolic hormones and elevates cortisol, working directly against the fat loss and shoulder development this protocol targets [Sell, Lukaszewski & Townsley 2017].

The rules
1Eat at ~2,262 kcal with 175 g protein every day. This single variable controls more of the outcome than any other.
2Train shoulders and upper back in every session. SWR is the metric furthest from target; no amount of fat loss closes that gap without direct structural work.
3Get 7.5–8 hours of sleep on a fixed schedule before any other recovery intervention. At 6–7 hours, cortisol dismantles the progress training builds.
4Never skip a session because it feels short. At 45 minutes, full-effort compound work is sufficient; consistency over 12 weeks outweighs any single session.
5Track waist circumference every two weeks, not weight. CWR is the most socially visible metric here, and the scale will mislead as shoulder mass replaces fat.

Bio rewrite

Current. "30 · Entrepreneur · Coffee, surf, dogs 🐕☕🌊 DM me ✨"

Proposed. "Run a software company in the day, lose to my dog at chess in the evening. Looking for someone who can hold a conversation about anything from architecture to bad horror films."

The proposed bio reveals what you actually do, what kind of person you are after work, and what kind of conversation you'd reward. Concrete > abstract, every time.

Opener templates
Anchor on a specific photo detail. "That climbing wall in photo 3 — was that Yosemite or the gym?" Beats every "hey" by a factor of 4x.
Ask a real question, not a compliment. "What's the bad horror film you'd defend in court?" Beats "you're stunning" by every metric.
Mirror their bio energy. If she's playful, be playful. If she's direct, be direct. Mismatched energy reads as inauthentic.
End with an easy out. "If you'd rather not talk about your day job, I get it." Lowers the cost of replying.
Never lead with "We should grab a drink." Closing the deal in the first message is the single most common rejection trigger.
